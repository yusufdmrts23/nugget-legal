Privacy Policy

Nugget: AI Flashcards & Tutor · Last updated 5 October 2026

Nugget is in development and not yet on the App Store. This policy describes the app as it is being built. Before release we will check it against the finished app, update it, and change the date above.

The short version

  • There is no account. Your decks, notes, cards, recordings and study history are stored on your iPhone, not on our servers.
  • Lecture recordings are transcribed on your iPhone by Apple's on-device speech recognition. Audio never leaves your phone.
  • Short material is turned into notes and cards on your iPhone. Longer material is sent as text to our server and on to an AI provider, Anthropic, only after you agree, and our server keeps none of it.
  • Our server keeps one thing: a daily count of how much each subscription used, for cost monitoring and abuse prevention.
  • No ads, no tracking, no selling of data, and nothing is used to train AI models.

1. Who we are

Nugget is published by Yusuf Demirtaş, an individual developer, who is the data controller for the purposes of this policy. You can reach us at tiko23miko23@gmail.com.

2. There is no account, but there are identifiers

Nugget does not ask you to register. It never asks for your name, email address, phone number, address or contacts. During setup it asks for your date of birth only to confirm you are 13 or older (section 15); the answer stays on your phone.

Some per-installation identifiers still exist, and we would rather list them than let "no account" suggest there is nothing:

  • A subscription identifier. Our subscription provider, RevenueCat, gives the app an anonymous identifier: a random string with no name or contact details in it. It is the only identifier our server ever sees. It lets our server check that a free trial or subscription is active before doing any AI work, and it keys the daily usage count in section 6. It is still a persistent identifier, and the privacy laws of several places treat it, and everything tied to it, as personal information. So do we.
  • Analytics and crash-report identifiers. PostHog and Sentry (section 9) each create their own random identifier for the installation.
  • Apple's identifier for vendor. Nugget's own code does not read it, but RevenueCat's software sends it to RevenueCat with its requests (section 8). It is not the advertising identifier and needs no permission.

Nugget never reads Apple's advertising identifier and never asks to track you.

3. What stays on your phone

Everything you make in Nugget is stored in the app's own storage on your iPhone: your folders, decks, notes, cards, images, recordings, transcripts, the text read from documents and photos you feed in, your review history and schedule, your streak, XP, Pals and exam plans, and your settings. We cannot see any of it.

Because it lives only on your phone, it is protected by your phone's passcode and iOS data protection, and it is included in your iPhone's iCloud or computer backup like the rest of your apps' data; that is how it moves to a new iPhone. A setting lets you leave recordings out of backups. You can also save everything to a file of your own with Export everything in Settings, and restore it from that file.

If you delete the app, or use Delete all data in Settings, this data is gone from the phone. We have no copy and cannot restore it.

4. Recording lectures: audio never leaves your phone

When you record a lecture, the audio is saved on your iPhone and transcribed on the iPhone by Apple's on-device speech recognition (the Speech framework). The audio is not sent to us, to Apple or to anyone else. The language files that speech recognition needs may be downloaded from Apple the first time you use a language; that download sends nothing you recorded.

The transcript is text, and it is treated like any other text you feed in (section 5). Audio is kept until you delete it; deleting a recording's audio keeps its transcript.

Recording other people may need their permission where you are, and your school may have its own rules on recording lectures. Our Terms of Use ask you to follow them.

5. Making notes and cards: on your phone first, the cloud only with your consent

Nugget uses AI for two jobs: turning a transcript, document, photos or pasted text into structured notes ("Clean up"), and turning notes into flashcards. It does them in one of two places.

On your iPhone

On iPhones that support Apple Intelligence, shorter material is processed by Apple's on-device language model. Nothing is sent anywhere. Text in photos and scanned pages is always read on the iPhone with Apple's text recognition before anything else happens.

In the cloud, after you agree

Longer material, and everything on iPhones without Apple Intelligence, is processed in the cloud. The first time that is needed, Nugget shows a consent screen that says what will be sent and to whom. Nothing is sent until you agree, and you can withdraw consent at any time in Settings; Nugget then works only on the phone.

What is sent, for each request:

  • a section of text: part of a transcript, of a document's text, of text read from photos, of text you pasted, or of your notes;
  • the options you chose, such as how many cards to make, the card types and the language;
  • the subscription identifier from section 2, so our server can check your subscription.

Pictures are sent only in one case: a handwritten page that the phone could not read reliably, and only if you allow it for that page. The picture is then sent so the AI can read the handwriting.

The text goes to our server (section 6), which checks your subscription and passes the text, without the identifier, to our AI provider (section 7). The notes or cards that come back are shown to you in a review tray; nothing is saved until you accept it, and it is then saved only on your phone.

Please do not feed in material containing other people's sensitive personal information (for example their health records) unless you have a right to and your study needs it.

6. What our server keeps

Our server is a single function hosted by Supabase. It keeps no notes, cards, transcripts, documents, pictures or results: it holds each request only in memory while it is answered. It writes exactly one kind of record:

RecordWhat it containsWhyHow long
Daily usage The subscription identifier, the date, how many sections of text were processed that day, how many AI tokens they used, and when the subscription was last checked To watch the cost of the AI service, and to stop automated abuse with a daily ceiling far above what a student uses Up to 12 months

To check a subscription, our server asks RevenueCat whether the identifier has an active trial or subscription and remembers the answer, in memory only, for up to an hour.

Supabase also keeps its own short-lived technical logs of requests to our server, such as the time, the response code and the IP address a request came from. They never contain what you sent, because our server never logs it. They expire on Supabase's schedule.

If you email us, your email and our reply stay in our support mailbox (section 8) until the matter is closed, and are deleted on request.

7. The AI provider

Cloud notes and cards are written by Anthropic's Claude models. We want to be exact here rather than reassuring:

  • We do not use your material to train AI models, and Anthropic's commercial terms state that it may not train models on customer content from its services.
  • Anthropic's documentation states that inputs and outputs are automatically deleted within 30 days, with exceptions: where a request is flagged under Anthropic's usage policy, it may be kept for up to two years, and trust-and-safety classification scores for up to seven; and longer where the law requires it.
  • Anthropic receives no identifier from us: nothing it is sent says which installation it came from, though the text itself may contain names or other details you fed in.

So "our server keeps nothing" is true of us, and up to thirty days at Anthropic is not zero. Deleting a deck on your phone does not delete Anthropic's copy, and Anthropic offers no published way to delete an individual request early.

9. Analytics and crash reports

Nugget sends product analytics through PostHog and crash and error reports through Sentry, so we can see which features are used and fix what breaks. Both can be switched off remotely by us, and Settings will let you switch them off on your phone.

PostHog receives event names with numbers and fixed codes, never free text: for example that the app was opened, which tab was selected, that a study session ended with how many cards were reviewed, or that something was shared, with the kind of material and the deck's size. Each event carries the app's version, the device model, the iOS version, the language and time zone, and the IP address the request came from, which PostHog can use to work out an approximate location. We do not use PostHog's screen recording, automatic capture of taps, or person profiles.

Sentry receives crash and error reports: what failed, short diagnostic messages chosen from a fixed list, and technical details about the app and the device, such as the app version, device model, iOS version, memory and language. We switch off Sentry's collection of personal information, screenshots and view hierarchies.

Never included in analytics or crash reports: note text, card text, transcripts, audio, document or photo contents, deck names, or what you sent to the AI.

12. Sharing what you make

You can export a deck, its notes or its cards as a file, a PDF or an image. Nothing leaves your phone until you tap Share and choose where it goes; the app never reads your contacts. A shared file contains the content you chose to share, which the people you send it to can read, and carries the Nugget name and a link to the App Store. A shared deck file does not include your review history or recordings.

13. Selling, sharing and advertising

We do not sell personal information, share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, show ads, or track you across other companies' apps and websites. Nugget contains no advertising software.

Audio recordings, and anything processed only on your phone, are not collected.

15. Children and teenagers

Nugget is for people aged 13 and over. During setup it asks for your date of birth and does not open for anyone under 13; the date stays on your phone and is never sent to us. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has used Nugget, write to us and we will delete what our server holds for that installation.

If you are under 18, or under the age of majority where you live, use Nugget with your parent's or guardian's permission. Where the law requires a parent's consent before your text is sent to an AI service, the consent screen in section 5 is for them to agree to.

16. Your rights and deleting your data

On your phone. Delete all data in Settings deletes your decks, notes, cards, media, recordings, transcripts, history and settings from the phone, and the app's entries in the iOS keychain. It does not cancel a subscription, which you manage through Apple (section 10).

On our server. The only thing our server holds is the daily usage record in section 6. To have it deleted, email us with the anonymous identifier the app shows in Settings; we will delete it and confirm. Anthropic's copy of what you sent cannot be found from that identifier and is deleted on Anthropic's schedule (section 7).

Other requests. Wherever you live, you can ask us whether we hold personal information about you, for a copy of it, to correct it or to delete it, and you can withdraw your consent to cloud processing at any time in Settings. Email tiko23miko23@gmail.com; we will not ask you to create an account to make a request. We answer within 30 days, and tell you if we need longer where the law allows it. If we decline a request, you can appeal by replying with "Appeal". Depending on where you live, you may also have rights to portability, to object to or restrict processing, and to complain to your data protection authority (in Brazil, the ANPD; in the EU and UK, your national authority). We will not treat you differently for making a request.

17. Where data is processed, and security

Text you send to the cloud passes through our server in India (Supabase, Mumbai) and is processed by our AI provider in the United States. RevenueCat, PostHog and Sentry operate in the United States. Where the law requires a safeguard for these transfers, we rely on our providers' standard contractual clauses or equivalent terms. Everything is sent encrypted (HTTPS/TLS). On your phone, Nugget's data is protected by iOS data protection. No transmission or storage is completely secure, and we cannot promise absolute security.
